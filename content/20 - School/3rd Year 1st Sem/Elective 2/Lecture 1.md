# 🎯 Introduction to AI and Intelligent Systems

As Herbert Simon noted, machines now possess the capability to think, learn, and create, with their problem-solving range rapidly expanding toward human-level capacity. **Intelligence** is broadly defined as the ability to **learn and understand**, **solve problems**, and make appropriate decisions.

## ❓ What is Artificial Intelligence (AI)?

AI is an **applied sub-field** combining computer science, physiology, and philosophy. Its primary goals include **empirical modeling** of intelligent systems, **theoretical exploration**, and **practical problem-solving** to design intelligent machines.

## ❓ The Turing Test

Alan Turing's foundational paper, “Computing Machinery and Intelligence,” introduced the **Turing Imitation Game** to address whether machines can think.

**Phase 1**: An interrogator attempts to distinguish a man from a woman through text-based questioning, with the man trying to deceive the interrogator.

**Phase 2**: The man is replaced by a computer programmed to mimic human-like responses and mistakes.

The Turing Test offers an **objective, experiment-independent standard** for evaluating machine intelligence and validating knowledge-based systems.

## 🔍 Classification of Intelligent Systems

Intelligent systems generally split into two categories:

*   **Computational Intelligence** (Data-Driven): Includes **Artificial Neural Networks** (brain-like neuron processing), **Fuzzy Logic** (managing uncertainty), and **Genetic Algorithms** (mimicking biological evolution).
*   **Classical Artificial Intelligence** (Logic-Driven): Includes traditional AI and **Knowledge-Based/Expert Systems** that apply expert-derived rules.

## 🔍 History of Artificial Intelligence

**Birth of AI (1943–1956)**: Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts proposed the first **artificial neural network model** in 1943. John von Neumann supported the creation of the first neural network computer in 1951, while Claude Shannon highlighted the necessity of **heuristics** in chess-playing machines. The 1956 Dartmouth workshop officially established Artificial Intelligence as a distinct science.


**The Rise of AI (1956–late 1960s)**: Frank Rosenblatt proved the **perceptron convergence theorem**, and Newell and Simon developed the **General Problem Solver (GPS)** using state space search, though it was eventually abandoned due to excessive computational requirements for real-world tasks.
